TILES (Tracking Individual Performance with Sensors) was a large-scale, longitudinal study
tracking hospital workers' physiology, environment, sleep, and behavior using wearable and
ambient sensors, in order to understand how individual differences, mental states, and
well-being affect job performance.

<img src="/images/projects/tiles/tiles-banner.jpg" alt="TILES study banner" style="width:100%; border-radius:8px; margin:1.25rem 0;" />

The study focused specifically on nurses and other healthcare professionals, since burnout rates
in that field are high and stand to reveal unique insights into how different individuals cope
with daily stress and continue to thrive in the workplace despite high physical and emotional
demands. Researchers at USC, the University of Washington, INRS-EMT/Université du Québec, and
Evidation Health partnered with the Intelligence Advanced Research Projects Activity (IARPA) to
carry out the study, which collected physiologic data (via wearable sensors), environmental data
(via ambient sensors), and behavioral data (via surveys) from a cohort of up to 250 nurses and
healthcare professionals over the course of 10 weeks at a time, day and night shifts included.

The project produced two publicly released datasets, TILES-2018 and TILES-2019 (see
[Publications](/publications)).
